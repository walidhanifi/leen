Leen

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 September 2026

Summary

Leen keeps your readings on your phone. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you sign in, and signing in is optional. If you sign in, your readings are backed up to a database in Sydney that only your account can read. There is no advertising, no analytics, no trackers, and no third party that receives your data.

What is stored on your phone

Everything the app records lives in a private database inside the app:

  • weight and bioimpedance from your scale, with the time of each reading
  • lengths from a Bluetooth tape measure, and the site each one was taken at
  • the people you have set up, and the details you gave them: name, height, birth date, sex, and whether the athlete equation applies
  • any DEXA scan you enter, including the note you wrote with it
  • the scales and tape measures the app has heard before, by name
  • a record of files you have imported: the file name and how many rows it held
  • your settings, such as units, goal weight and which measurement sites you show

The app also keeps a few recent copies of that database in its own folder, so a bad import can be undone, and writes a small summary of your latest numbers where the widgets can read it. Both stay on the phone.

Body fat, lean mass and the rest are not stored. They are worked out from your weight and impedance every time they are shown. The exception is a body fat figure that arrived inside an imported file, which is kept as it came.

Signing in

Sign in exists for one reason: so your history survives a lost phone and lands on the next one. Every part of the app works without it, and it can be left alone forever.

You can sign in with Apple, or with a link sent to your email address. There is no password. If you use Apple, you can hide your real address, and the app only ever sees what Apple passes on. If you use email, the sign in email is sent for us by Supabase.

What is uploaded when you are signed in

While you are signed in, the app copies these to your account and keeps them in step with the phone:

  • weight and bioimpedance for every reading, and when it was taken
  • body measurements: the site and the length
  • the people you have set up, and their name, height, birth date and sex
  • DEXA scans and their notes
  • the names of the scales and tape measures the app has heard, and when it last heard them
  • the names of files you imported and their row counts
  • your settings
  • your email address, if you signed in with email, or the identity Apple hands over, if you signed in with Apple
  • a user id, created with the account, which is what ties the rows above to you

That is the whole list. Readings the demo produces are never uploaded.

Where it is stored

In a Postgres database run by Supabase in the ap-southeast-2 region, which is Sydney, Australia. The region was fixed when the project was created and cannot be moved.

Every table carries a row level security policy that ties each row to a single user id and refuses to hand a row to anyone else. That rule is enforced by the database itself rather than by the app, so a mistake in the app cannot expose another person's readings. Supabase runs the database for us and does nothing else with what is in it.

Health information

Weight, bioimpedance and body measurements are health information, and health information is sensitive information under the Australian Privacy Act 1988. It is collected only with your consent, and it is used for one purpose: showing your own numbers back to you. It is not sold, not shared, not used to build a profile of you, and not sent to anyone for any other reason.

No advertising, no analytics

The app contains no advertising, no analytics, no trackers, no crash reporting and no third party SDK that sends anything anywhere. Nothing counts your taps. Nothing records that you opened the app. Your data is never sold or shared.

Two servers are contacted, and only these two. Supabase, when you are signed in, to hold the backup described above. Expo's update service, to ask whether a newer version of the app itself exists. That question carries the app version and the platform, and nothing about you or your readings.

Bluetooth

Bluetooth is used to hear your own scale and your own tape measure, and for nothing else. The reading they broadcast is decoded and stored on the phone. Nothing is sent out over Bluetooth, no other device is contacted, and Bluetooth is not used to work out where you are.

If a scale is heard but a reading never saves, Settings has a diagnostics file that describes what the radio did, including the weights it decoded and the device name. It is held in memory only while the app is open, it is never sent anywhere on its own, and you decide who you send it to.

Files you import

You can bring history in from Renpho, Withings, Fitbit, Garmin, Zepp, Eufy, openScale, or any CSV with a date column and a weight column. You pick the file yourself, and it is read on the phone. It is not uploaded anywhere to be processed. The readings inside it are then treated like any other reading.

The app has no connection to Apple Health and does not read Apple Health data.

Deleting your data

Before deleting anything, you can take it with you. Settings, Data, Export writes every reading, measurement, person and device to CSV or JSON, including the raw weight and impedance, so nothing is locked in here.

Delete account, in Settings, Account. This erases the account and every row under it in Sydney, then erases everything on the phone as well. It happens immediately, it cannot be undone, and there is no archive to restore from.

Delete everything, in Settings, Data. This clears this phone only. If you are signed in, your backup is untouched and the next sync brings it back down, so use Delete account when you want it gone for good.

Deleting the app. iOS removes the app's private storage with it, so every reading, backup copy and setting on the phone goes. If you were signed in, your backup stays in Sydney until you delete the account, which is what lets you reinstall, sign in and get your history back.

Children

Leen is not directed at children and is not intended for anyone under 16. Nothing is knowingly collected from a child. If a child has signed in, email the address below and the account will be deleted.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the current version is always at this address. A change to what leaves your phone will be said plainly in the app before it takes effect.

Contact

Questions about this policy, a privacy complaint, or a request for a copy of what is held about you: whanifi@medbill.com.au

Under the Australian Privacy Act you can ask for access to your personal information and ask for it to be corrected. Export in the app gives you the same thing straight away, but ask if you would rather it was sent to you. If a complaint is not resolved to your satisfaction, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner can review it, at oaic.gov.au.
